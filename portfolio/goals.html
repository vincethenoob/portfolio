Timeline

Short, Mid & Long-Term Goals

Ordered because the plan genuinely builds on itself — each stage assumes the one before it.

01
Short-Term — 1 to 2 Years

Land a Junior Developer Role & Close Core Gaps

  • Graduate and complete at least one industry certification (AWS Cloud Practitioner or equivalent).
  • Secure a junior full-stack or backend developer position, ideally converting from an internship.
  • Get comfortable deploying and monitoring a small production application, not just building it locally.
  • Build a portfolio of 3–4 polished, deployed projects beyond coursework.
02
Mid-Term — 3 to 5 Years

Grow Into a Mid-Level / Specialist Role

  • Specialize in either backend systems or data engineering, based on where my strengths land by then.
  • Take ownership of a feature or service end-to-end, including its cloud infrastructure.
  • Mentor at least one junior developer or intern, formalizing what I already enjoy doing informally.
  • Contribute to or maintain an open-source project relevant to my specialty.
03
Long-Term — 7 to 10 Years

Move Into Technical Leadership

  • Take on a technical lead or engineering manager role, guiding architecture decisions for a product team.
  • Have deep, teachable expertise in cloud infrastructure and secure system design.
  • Stay hands-on enough to still ship code, rather than moving fully into management.
  • Possibly return to mentor IT students the way I hope to be mentored early in my career.

Reflection

Motivation & Values

I got into IT because I liked the feeling of building something small that actually worked — a script that saved someone an afternoon of manual data entry, a scraper that pulled together research I would have otherwise done by hand. That feeling hasn't changed; it's just gotten more ambitious.

I want a career built on systems that are dependable, well-documented, and honest about their limitations — not just impressive in a demo.

I'm drawn to fintech and healthcare IT specifically because mistakes there have real consequences for real people, which pushes me to be more careful, not less ambitious. Long-term, I want to be someone junior developers can learn from — partly because I've benefited from mentors doing that for me, and partly because I think teaching something is the fastest way to actually understand it.
